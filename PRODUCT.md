# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People and technology teams in Indigenous Australian organisations who connect and administer laptops, field devices, servers, sites, and services. They may work from an office, on Country, or across unreliable networks. Their primary job is to understand what is connected, reach authorised machines, and make safe device changes without terminal-only workflows or repeated account switching.

## Product Purpose

BlakTail provides an open, self-hosted private path between an organisation's devices while keeping identity, policy, keys, and operational data under organisational control and onshore when deployed as documented. Success means a person can sign in once, see every machine they are authorised to access across multiple network workspaces, connect or diagnose the local Mac, and administer endpoints with clear scope and consequences.

## Brand Personality

Calm, sovereign, and practical. BlakTail should feel trustworthy under pressure, locally grounded, and technically exact without becoming cold or intimidating.

## Anti-references

- Generic VPN apps that reduce the product to a single unexplained on/off switch.
- Cyberpunk security styling, neon network maps, fake terminal decoration, and fear-based copy.
- Tailscale's one-active-account client model and any workflow that requires logout/login churn to see machines across authorised networks.
- Copies of Tailscale trademarks, wording, layouts, or visual assets.
- Dense administrative surfaces that hide network scope or make destructive actions easy to trigger accidentally.

## Design Principles

1. Show the whole authorised path. One session reveals every accessible network and machine while keeping each action scoped to its owning workspace.
2. Make connection state undeniable. Pair status words with familiar symbols and explain errors with a concrete recovery action.
3. Preserve identity. Friendly names are editable, while technical hostnames, MagicDNS names, and cryptographic identity remain visible and stable.
4. Keep common work native. Use standard macOS windows, menus, sidebars, search, keyboard shortcuts, settings, and accessibility behaviour.
5. Put sovereignty in the workflow. Show which organisation owns a machine and where the console and coordinator live without turning the interface into marketing copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for macOS. Support keyboard-only use and VoiceOver, label controls and status symbols, never rely on colour alone, preserve legibility at larger text sizes, respect Reduce Motion, use system contrast and accent choices, and keep pointer targets at or above the macOS minimum size. Use Australian English throughout.
